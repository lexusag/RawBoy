# RawBoy Desktop (macOS, in development)

RawBoy's editor as a native Mac app (SwiftUI + Metal, the iOS engine). Phase 1 is "RawBoy at iOS level + edits in sidecars + keyboard and mouse". Needs Apple Silicon (the Swift engine uses `Float16`) and macOS 15.

## Photos and sidecars
- Open with ⌘O, drop a file on the window, or Finder's Open With (which opens Browse). RAW, HEIC, JPEG, PNG and TIFF open in place (never copied).
- Edits are written to the photo's XMP sidecar as you work: `rawboy:Develop`, the same JSON as RawBoy on the web. The file is NAME.xmp next to the photo, else darktable's NAME.EXT.xmp, else a new NAME.xmp. Only that field changes; everything else in an existing sidecar is kept. Writes happen 800 ms after the last change, when another photo opens, when the app goes to the background and on quit. Quitting (or closing the window) while an export, Auto Dev, Auto's batch or the Packer is running, or after a sidecar write failed, asks first (Cancel / Quit Anyway; `QuitWork`).
- A photo with a sidecar reopens with its edits. One without takes the previous photo's edits (as on the phone). RawBoy Viewer's rotation (`tiff:Orientation`) and crop are read; RawBoy's own turn wins.
- Healing brush strokes are saved in the sidecar too; web-only values (portrait retouch and others the Mac app doesn't know) are kept untouched.

## Export
⌘E: JPEG or TIFF, into the photo's folder or a folder you choose, named NAME-rawboy.jpg / .tif (an existing file of that name is replaced).

## Window
One bar over the photo (the photo's name and exposure; Crop, Masks, Light, Heal, Presets, **Looks** (click it, then the photo: every LUT and film stock over that spot as labelled stripes, click one to use it, as on the web); Auto Dev, To Auto, Reset, Undo, Redo, Export) and the slider panel on the right: sections that open and close (Light, Curve, Colour, Primaries, Detail, Portrait, Effects, LUT, Lens; Curve starts closed, like Primaries, Portrait, Effects, LUT and Lens), each with an amber dot when it has edits and a reset for that section only. Under the photo, a **filmstrip** of the other photos in its folder (name order, a RAW + JPEG pair once): click one, or ← / →, to open the next. The phones keep their tabs.

## Keys and mouse
Z 100% at the pointer, Tab or F photo only (hides the bar and the panel; Tab stays with Full Keyboard Access when that is on), D frame colour, C Crop & rotate (drag outside the frame to rotate), \ Compare, Y before/after slider (drag the divider: as shot on the left, the edit on the right), B healing brush (inside Masks: brush), E / V masks, [ ] brush size, Delete removes the selected healing stroke, Esc or Return closes the tool, Space pans, ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z, ⌘O, ⌘E, ⌘/ help. Keys are matched by physical position. The wheel zooms around the pointer; over a slider it moves it (Shift big, ⌘ fine); double-click resets a slider.

## Displays and accessibility
- **Displays.** The window's frame is restored at launch; if its display is gone, or one is unplugged or changes resolution while RawBoy is open, the window moves onto the main screen (cut down to fit if the screen is smaller; `WindowPlacement`). Full screen and Zoom are the system's and use the window's own screen. The photo is drawn tagged **sRGB**, so macOS converts it for whichever display the window is on (wide-gamut or not) and for each one as the window is dragged between them; it redraws when the window changes screen, backing scale or colour profile. The preview is SDR: no EDR/HDR output (an HDR display shows it at SDR white).
- **VoiceOver.** Sliders say their name and value ("Exposure, +0.50 EV"), with a Reset action and a hint; value readouts are buttons ("Reset Exposure, now +0.50 EV"); the tone mapper, mask, leak, effect, look and LUT choices are buttons that say selected; section titles are headings; grid photos say name, status and look, with Select/Deselect actions; the photo is "Developed photo"; messages (toasts) are announced. The mouse-only handles (masks, curve) are described as groups and the sliders do the same from the keyboard.
- **Keyboard.** With Full Keyboard Access (System Settings › Keyboard), Tab moves through the controls with a focus ring and Space presses one. Auto's grid keeps its own arrows + Return focus.
- **Reduce Motion** turns the few fades and scrolls off; **Increase Contrast** gives chips a firmer outline and fill. Not done: the mouse handles can't be moved from the keyboard (the sliders can), the VoiceOver wording was not heard on a real VoiceOver, and Differentiate Without Colour isn't handled (selected chips differ by outline and fill, and say "selected" to VoiceOver).

## Masks: Lens blur
The Masks panel's **Lens blur** slider (Adjust tab, 0..100): a round, lens-like defocus inside the layer's mask, up to 3% of the long side, where small bright lights become clean bokeh discs and the sharp part next to the mask is never smeared. The same two Metal passes as the web's two GL passes (mask radius, then disc gather) on a highlight-boosted 1536 px copy; preview, 100% and export agree. See [rawboy.md](rawboy.md), Masks.

## Film scan 1.5×
The export sheets (Pro's and Auto's) offer **Film scan** (Off / 1.5× size) when a film look is on: the photo is rendered 1.5× larger, upscaled by iterative back-projection and lightly sharpened before the grain, which is generated at the finer pitch (`ScanScale.grain` 0.86 keeps its print-size strength); the size setting then doesn't apply. It is the web's and Android's feature, ported: `shaders/scan.frag` (the very same shader, built into `scan.metal` by `gen.sh`), `metal/ScanUpscaler.swift` (six passes in float targets), `ScanScale`, `OffscreenDeveloper.render(scale:)`, `Exporter.renderBands(scale:)`. The setting belongs to the export settings, remembered with them (not stored per photo or in the `rawboy:Auto` sidecar, as on the web). Same on the iPhone.

## Packer
**File › Pack Raws…** (⇧⌘P) opens RawBoy Packer in its own window: camera raws and DNGs re-saved as DNGs, with the same code (`dng_pack.cpp`, the Adobe DNG SDK, LibRaw), presets, formats, summary text and "Opens in" table as the web's Packer (see [packer.md](packer.md)). Drop or add raws and folders (grouped by folder, folders in the order added); **Sort** switches each folder between shoot date and file name (remembered, `packer.sort`), a click on a folder's header folds it to one line with its progress ("2 packing… · 1 done", "all done"), Collapse all / Expand all do every folder and ✕ removes one (as the web's Packer); each is read when added (camera and lens, raw data line, lens corrections in the file). **Pack as** Smallest files (Small raw), Maximum compatibility (Lossless) or Tiny copies (Micro, longest side), with Lens corrections, Keep location and Settings-in-the-file-name; Advanced: file type, quality, compression effort, preview and files at once (Auto: a third of the cores, one under 8 GB). It saves next to each file or into a folder, never over an existing file (`-1`, `-2` are added; DNG sources get `-packed`, or the settings with that option), and never touches the originals. Files run in parallel, each on its own big-stack thread; progress is per file, and Stop ends the run after the files being packed (the encoder can't be interrupted midway). Sizes on the same files match the web's within 4% (the preview JPEG differs: ImageIO here, jpegli there), and the packed DNGs decode to the same picture as the web's. macOS slice only: the iPhone libraries don't include the Packer.

## Auto mode
The **Pro | Auto** switch in the title bar (⌘1, ⌘2) turns the window into RawBoy Auto, the batch app of the iPhone (its SwiftUI screens are shared: `ios/Auto/`): add photos with ⌘O, a folder with ⇧⌘O, by dropping them on the window, or `RawBoy Desktop --auto a.RAF folder/` (`--import-as looks|film|plain` for how; RawBoy Viewer's Send to RawBoy Auto uses the `rawboy-desktop://auto?as=…&path=…` link and asks Looks, Film or As is); each is analysed and developed automatically with a suggested look (Looks or Film), shown in a grid of even cells with each photo whole and its name and look under it, beside a sidebar with the batch settings (New photos as Looks, Film or **As is** (no auto develop and no look; raws get their measured denoise, other files nothing), Order, Preset, Lens, a **Filter** by camera, lens, ISO range, shoot day and Unedited that the grid, the photo view's arrows and Export all follow, Auto denoise all, and the batch's count); open one for Pro's layout: a one-row bar (Grid, the photo's name and place in the batch, Previous / Next, Crop, Preset, Edit in Pro, **Copy** previous, **Apply to All**, Remove, Undo, Redo, Export) and a panel of sections that open and close (Look: Looks / Film, Strength and the looks as thumbnails; Light; Colour; Detail; **Retouch**, the web's natural portrait retouch on top of the look or preset, kept as the sidecar's `retouch` adjustment and copied by Apply to All and Copy), with the photo on its own (no pager) and its ISO in the bottom-left corner (the grid shows it beside each name);  a RawBoy preset from the ones saved in Pro (Preset; files are imported in Pro's Presets, which works with no photo open) and lens presets; **Export all** (⌘E) writes JPEG or TIFF next to each photo or into the folder you choose, named by the export sheet's **File name** template (the web's `NameTemplate`: `{name}`, `{camera}`, `{lens}`, `{iso}`, `{look}`, `{date}`, `{time}`, `{year}`…, `#` counters; default `{name}-auto`, remembered as `auto.nameTemplate`), never over an existing file (`-001`, `-002` are added); the progress shows the time elapsed and an estimate of the time left. Photos are used in place, never copied; "Remove" only takes a photo out of the batch. Keys: arrows move through the grid and between photos (Space and ⌫ too in a photo, as on the web), Return opens, ⌦ (fn-Delete) takes the open photo out of the batch, C Crop & rotate (C, Return or Esc put it down), ⇧C Copy previous, the wheel zooms the photo and moves the slider under the pointer, ⌘A selects all, ⌘-click toggles, ⇧-click selects the range from the last clicked photo, Esc clears the selection (then goes back from a photo), ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z undo, ⌘↩ **Edit in Pro** (the open or selected photo opens in the editor with its Auto develop as the starting edits, as the web's Edit in RawBoy), ⇧⌘A **Send to Auto** (also the **To Auto** button in the editor's bar: the photo goes back to Auto with Pro's edits, which develop it there in place of its look, Auto's sliders on top, **Use a look** drops them; kept in `rawboy:Auto` as the web's `edits`, and exported through the session so masks, lens blur and healing strokes come out). **Clear batch** (button, Mode menu) asks first and only takes photos out of the batch. A photo handed over by RawBoy Viewer always opens in the editor, even when Auto is showing. A photo or folder from the Finder (Open With, a Dock drop) always opens in Browse, whichever mode was showing; editing starts there (E, Send to Auto). Desktop's document types are rank Alternate: listed under Open With and selectable as the default, but never the default on its own. Each photo's settings are kept in its XMP sidecar, `rawboy:Auto` (800 ms after a change; the web's `AutoSidecar` JSON, `AutoSidecar.swift`), next to `rawboy:Develop`; adding the photo again restores them, RawBoy Web and the Browser read the same field. Parts of the web's Auto JSON this engine has no counterpart for are kept and written back. Paging is quick on the Mac: once a photo is open, the next one is decoded ahead at full resolution and the cached previews of the next three and previous two are read ahead with their LUT or film stock (the web's `NeighbourPreload` and `prefetchNext`; machines that analyse in one lane, such as iPhones, decode only the photo shown). Skin analysis uses the Vision-based mask (below). Differences from the iPhone app: no Photos library (files only); exports go to a folder.

**Reset all photos…** (toolbar next to Clear batch, and Mode menu) puts every photo back to one look, one film stock or **As is** (the Natural look: the automatic exposure and tone only), asking first with the photo count. It clears each photo's Strength, brightness, warmth, tint, contrast, saturation, grain, denoise, rotation, straighten, crop and any RawBoy preset; the file list, the lens preset choice and the auto develop stay, each photo gets its own undo step (Undo in its photo view) and its sidecar is written as for any edit. The look or stock a photo has is named on its grid cell (hover for the whole text) and in bold above the look strip in the photo view.

Auto analyses up to three photos side by side (a third of the cores, one lane per 6 GB of memory), and keeps each photo's analysis (small copies, lens data, noise, faces) in `~/Library/Caches/<app id>/analysis` (up to 1 GB, keyed by file path, size and modified time), so a folder added again isn't decoded again; the develop itself is worked out again each time. `RAWBOY_NO_ANALYSIS_CACHE=1` turns the cache off. A photo restored from it has no big detail preview until it is opened (the grid thumbnail stands in).

## Browse mode

The third mode, ⌘3 (Pro | Auto | Browse), is the one RawBoy Desktop starts in on every launch (the last mode is not remembered): RawBoy Viewer's browser (grid, sidebar, culling) and viewer inside the window,
from the Viewer's libraries (`lexusag/RawBoyImageViewer`: RawBoyBrowseKit, ViewerFiles, ViewerImaging, ViewerUI; see "Where the Viewer's libraries come from" below). Desktop owns the
`AppModel` (`DesktopModel.browse`) with `ViewerOptions`: the menu bar and Dock stay (`hidesDockAndMenuBar` off), the viewer is
a view over the grid (`viewerInHostWindow`), and its preferences are kept under `desktop.browse.` (`ViewerSettings.store`).
ViewerUI installs its own key monitor while Browse shows; Desktop's `KeyboardShortcuts` only takes ⌘1/⌘2 there. The Browse
menu is the Viewer's Photo menu (`ViewerPhotoCommands`), on while Browse shows. Browse's settings (the Viewer's Settings window, `ViewerSettingsRoot`) are the app's Settings… (⌘,); they apply to Browse only, except **Interface size** (85–150 %), which sizes the whole app: Pro's bar, panel and filmstrip, Auto's grid, sidebar, bar and panel, and the Packer window follow it as soon as it changes (`DesktopModel.interfaceScale` → `EnvironmentValues.interfaceScale` → `interfaceScaled()`, the Viewer's own lay-out-smaller-then-scale method). The photo is never scaled, so it stays sharp at every size. `Desktop/DesktopHandOff.swift`
implements the Viewer's `PhotoHandOff` in-process: E in the viewer opens the photo in Pro (whichever mode was showing; Auto's
batch is kept), Send to RawBoy Auto adds photos or folders to Auto's batch as Looks, Film or As is. **As is** from Browse
brings each photo with its sidecar and nothing else: its `rawboy:Auto` settings if it has them, else its RawBoy edits
(`rawboy:Develop`: the Viewer's crop and turn, Pro's edits) developing it in place of a look, else the file as it is;
no measured denoise, batch preset or lens preset is added (`AutoViewModel.add(sidecarOnly:)`, `SidecarEdits.develop`). Real previews (R, and Compare's) are the
engine's as-shot develop (`Desktop/EngineRealPreview.swift`, a `RealPreviewProvider` given to ViewerUI through
`ViewerHost.realPreview` before the model is made): `RawImage.open`, the as-shot `DevelopParams` with no edits, then
`Exporter.renderCGImage` (full-resolution bands developed on the GPU and area-downscaled to the requested size), so the
picture is Pro's and pixel-identical to a `PhotoSession` export at that size; 8-bit sRGB (Core Image's is Display P3). One develop at
a time, the GPU developer dropped 20 s after the last request, a sidecar's orientation applied once afterwards
(`ImageLoader.oriented`), Core Image for anything the engine refuses and for non-raws. Timings at 3440 px on this Mac
(cold / warm, engine vs Core Image): Sony 24 MP DNG 1.1 / 1.0 s vs 1.9 / 1.8 s; Sony 24 MP ARW 0.55 / 0.55 s vs 0.34 / 0.19 s;
Sony 33 MP ARW 0.75 / 0.70 s vs 0.33 / 0.18 s; Fujifilm GFX 102 MP RAF 2.4 / 2.2 s vs 0.9 / 0.6–1.6 s. The engine decodes the
whole raw (no half-size draft decode in the Swift API), Core Image develops ARW/RAF at a reduced scale; `EngineRealPreviewTests.testTimings`
(`RAWBOY_PREVIEW_TIMING=<files>`) measures it again.
Ratings, picks, turns and crops are the same `xmp:Rating` / `rawboy:Pick` / `tiff:Orientation` / `rawboy:Develop` fields Pro and
Auto use, through the Kit's `XmpSidecar`. The viewer is in this window by default; **Mode › Full-Screen Viewer in Browse** (remembered as `desktop.browse.viewer.immersive`)
opens the Viewer's screen-filling borderless window instead, with its edge panels, press-and-hold peek and the menu bar and
Dock hidden while it is the key window (`ViewerOptions.hidesDockAndMenuBar` is on for those windows only; the in-window viewer
never hides anything), and ⌥⌘↩ (Open Selection in the Other Viewer) opens one selection the other way. Both open on the display
this window is on (`opensOnHostScreen`); Compare follows the same choice (`CompareView` in the window, or its own window).
E in the full-screen viewer hands the photo to Pro (`DesktopHandOff`), closes that window and brings the window and the menu
bar back; leaving Browse (⌘1/⌘2) closes any window of Browse's own (`AppModel.closeOwnWindows`). The presentation options
are restored when the window closes, the app resigns active or quits, and when a display is unplugged the window moves to
the display it overlaps most, else the main one, and fills it (`ScreenWatch`). The Desktop window itself is untouched. Needs
a human on real displays: a second display, unplugging one while the viewer is full screen, "Displays have separate Spaces"
on and off (the menu bar of the other display), and Mission Control. Debug `RAWBOY_BROWSE_PROBE=immersive` (calls ViewerUI directly through its `#if DEBUG` helpers; it never posts key events, which go through the system and can reach other apps).
Launch with `--browse <folder or photo>`; debug `RAWBOY_BROWSE_PROBE=1` (copies
only) selects all, rates, and exercises both hand-offs. The Kit also replaced the engine's own `XmpSidecar`, `PhotoOrder`
(`CaptureDate` stays in `auto/CaptureDate.swift`) and the filmstrip's folder scan and thumbnails (`Desktop/ProFilmstrip.swift`).

## Where the Viewer's libraries come from

`ios/project.yml`'s `packages:` block (between the `viewer-package` markers) says, and `ios/viewer-source.sh` switches it:

- `ios/viewer-source.sh local [path]`: a sibling checkout, `../../RawBoyImageViewer/Packages`. For
  development, with the Viewer branch you are working on; the libraries are built with `-O` even in Debug.
- `ios/viewer-source.sh git` (**the committed default**): `https://github.com/lexusag/RawBoyImageViewer` `from: 0.2.3` (the pin is `VIEWER_FROM` in the script). SwiftPM
  needs a `Package.swift` at the root of the repository: v0.2.0 has none, **v0.2.1 is the first tag that has**. A git checkout cannot use
  `unsafeFlags`, so Debug builds run the Viewer's libraries unoptimised (Release is optimised; measured). To try the git route before a
  tag: `VIEWER_URL=file:///path/to/RawBoyImageViewer VIEWER_BRANCH=<branch> ios/viewer-source.sh git` (done once: the Desktop build resolves it).

Run `xcodegen` after switching. The project has been on `git` since v0.2.1 was tagged: CI (`ci.yml` desktop job) and the release job
(`release.yml`) let SwiftPM fetch the tag and cache `SourcePackages` by the pin; their "RawBoy Viewer libraries" step clones
`VIEWER_REF` beside the repository only if project.yml is `local`. To raise the pin, change `VIEWER_FROM` in the script, run it, commit
`ios/project.yml`. Develop against a local Viewer with `ios/viewer-source.sh local` (do not commit it).

## Skin: Skin retouch, Blemish removal, Portrait retouch
MediaPipe has no macOS build, so the Mac finds faces and skin with Apple's Vision framework (nothing bundled, no third-party code). Each face's skin is the polygon of Vision's face contour plus a forehead cap above the eyebrows, minus eyes, eyebrows, lips and nostrils (from Vision's 76 landmarks), cut back by a skin-colour model sampled from the face itself; neck, arms and hands are found by that colour model inside Vision's person mask (weight 0.85). How it differs from MediaPipe's (iOS, Android, web): there is no trained skin class, so a garment or hair close to the skin's tone can be taken for skin and skin in very different light to the face's is missed; the forehead's top edge is an estimate (Vision has no hairline); Vision finds fewer small or side-on faces (the mask is made on a 1280 px copy), and a dark scene is tried at several exposures until a face is found. **Skin retouch**, **Blemish removal** and **Portrait retouch** (Portrait section; per mask layer too, in the Masks panel) run on that mask. Portrait retouch is the web's algorithm and numbers (`PortraitRetouch.swift`; face landmarks reach it through `FaceShape`): spots, under-eyes, lines and evening, as a gains map the develop shader applies, so preview, 100% and export match. iOS: the engine is shared, but MediaPipe's landmarks aren't handed over yet, so the Portrait retouch sliders are Mac-only for now.

## Masks: Lens blur
Heal in the top bar, or B: click a dust spot, blemish or small object to heal it away, drag to paint a stroke. Healed in the full-resolution image by seamless cloning (the same native `healStroke` as the web): the tone comes from around it, the detail from a clean area the engine picks (**Auto source**) or one you place (**Choose source**: drag the dashed copy; new strokes keep that offset). Size and Feather apply to the next strokes; pick a stroke in the list to change its source or delete it; up to 64 strokes per photo, reset for each new photo and kept out of presets. Strokes are undone one at a time with ⌘Z. The iPhone build has the engine but no button yet (its top bar is full).

## Lens corrections
Lens section › **Use Lensfun profile**: distortion, chromatic aberration and vignetting from the Lensfun database for raws with no embedded profile, as on the web (Auto match from EXIF, Pick lens, Generic). See [rawboy.md](rawboy.md), Lens corrections. The database is inside the app; nothing is downloaded.

## Light leaks: outline dots
A glow leak (Light tool) shows 8 hollow dots on its outline: drag one with the mouse to reshape the outline around what it should hide (0.1–4 shape radii, a closed spline through the 8 points, as on the web); the size dots then sit on the reshaped outline. **Round outline** in the panel puts the ellipse back. The radii are saved in the sidecar like the rest of the leak.

## Not in yet
a folder browser, Intel Macs, sandboxing and notarisation (the app writes next to your photos, so it is not sandboxed).

## The DMG
Publishing a release builds `RawBoy-Desktop-<tag>.dmg` (the `desktop` job of `.github/workflows/release.yml`, on a macOS runner: macOS native slice with `ios/native/build.sh macos`, xcodegen, `xcodebuild` Release, ad-hoc signed, app + an Applications link packed with `hdiutil`) and attaches it to the release. It is not notarised: open the app the first time with right-click > Open (or System Settings > Privacy & Security > Open Anyway). To rebuild only the DMG for an existing release, run the workflow by hand with that tag and tick **Skip building the APKs**; **Skip building RawBoy Desktop's DMG** leaves it out. The job uses the newest non-beta Xcode on the `macos-26` runner.
**Version.** The Help sheet (the "?" button, ⌘/) starts with "RawBoy Desktop <version> (<build>)", and the Help menu has a disabled "About RawBoy Desktop <version> (<build>)" line above the Help item (the standard About panel is still in the app menu). The numbers are the bundle's: the release workflow passes the tag as `MARKETING_VERSION` and its run number as `CURRENT_PROJECT_VERSION`, and `Desktop-Info.plist` follows them (before, it held a literal 1.0 (1), so a DMG always said 1.0); a local build says 1.0 (1).
