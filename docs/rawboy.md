# RawBoy controls

## Controls

Upright, the controls sit under the photo; turn the phone sideways (or use a tablet or a wide window) and they
move to a panel on the right, using its whole height. Android, iPhone and the web.

| Slider | What it does |
|---|---|
| Tone mapper | **AgX** (default), **Filmic** or **Sigmoid**, per photo (carries over, in the Light preset group). All three use AgX's log range and put middle grey, display white and the slope at grey in the same place, so the sliders below mean the same and switching doesn't change brightness. AgX: Rec.2020 inset/outset, highlights desaturate towards white. Filmic (after darktable's filmic rgb): straight latitude ~1.3 EV each side of grey with rational toe and shoulder, on max RGB with the colour ratios kept, fading to white across the shoulder; slightly lifted deep shadows. Sigmoid (after darktable's sigmoid): a logistic curve in EV per channel with hue preservation; keeps more colour in bright highlights. RawBoy Auto and Camera always use AgX (their film looks are built on it). **Film**: the photo developed as negative + print on a stock (the same 12 stocks and grain/halation as Auto's Film mode), with **Stock**, **Film strength** (blends with the AgX/Filmic/Sigmoid render picked before), **Film grain** (default 75) and **Halation** (0..200, 100 = the stock's own, faint as on real film; above 100 it doubles every 25, 16x at 200; only light sources and highlights at the current exposure halate, so a photo without them shows little change). Picking AgX, Filmic or Sigmoid turns the film off; Film brings the last stock back. A photo sent from Auto's Film mode opens with Film selected; presets (Light group) and carry-over keep it |
| Exposure | ±5 EV, scene-linear gain before tone mapping |
| Contrast | slope of the tone curve's log encoding around middle grey |
| Highlights | luminance gain on tones above middle grey, up to ±2 EV. **Negative = recover highlight detail** |
| Shadows | luminance gain on tones below middle grey, up to ±2 EV. **Positive = lift shadow detail** |
| Whites | **+ lowers the white point** (brighter whites, clips sooner), − dims whites |
| Blacks | **− raises the black point** (deeper blacks), + lifts/fades blacks |
| Curves | Light tab, under the sliders: point curves on the final sRGB image (after the LUT), like Lightroom's point curve. **RGB** acts on all three channels, then **Red**, **Green** and **Blue** each have their own curve (a dot marks the ones in use). Tap the box to add a point (up to 16 per curve), drag a point to move it (it stays between its neighbours; the end points move too, for a faded black or a dimmed white), double-tap a point to remove it, **Reset** straightens the curve shown. With touch, only a press on the curve or a point edits it (a new point lands on the curve); elsewhere in the box the panel scrolls. The curve between points is a monotone cubic, so it never overshoots them. Carries over to the next photo and has its own **Curves** preset group; RawBoy Auto renders a preset's curves too |
| Temperature / Tint | Bradford re-balance relative to the as-shot white. Defaults are read from the file. Like Lightroom: + Temperature = warmer, + Tint = more magenta. Ranges 1000–12000 K and ±250 tint (wider than Lightroom's, so infrared photos can be neutralised; below 1667 K the locus follows Krystek's approximation, joined to Kim's) |
| Pick white balance / Auto WB | Color tab: tap (click on the web) something neutral grey in the photo and Temperature/Tint are set so it renders grey. Averages a square of 1% of the short side of the decoded image (linear, before any develop step, through crop/rotation/lens), leaving out clipped pixels; works in the fit and the 100% view. **Auto WB** shows a draggable, extendable rectangle over the fit view and solves Temperature/Tint so the non-clipped pixels inside render neutral grey. **Reset WB** reverts Temperature and Tint back to the file's as-shot defaults |
| Saturation | scene-linear saturation before AgX, limited by purity (after Saulala's *Sat vs Sat*): neutrals and moderate colours get the full boost, colours already near the Rec.2020 edge less, so they are never clipped flat |
| RGB primaries | After darktable's *rgb primaries*: hue (±45°) and purity (×0.5–×2) per Rec.2020 primary, plus a white-point tint. One 3×3 matrix in scene-linear light (combined with white balance), white stays white. Reimplemented from the method, not darktable's GPL code |
| Luma denoise | 0–100. Multi-scale (wavelet) denoise of luminance: noise is taken out at every scale from 1 px to about 60 px, against the noise camera raws carry at each scale, and an even fine grain of the photo's own noise is left, so flat walls and skies stay smooth without blotchy texture when the exposure goes up. Works in a variance-stabilised space (sqrt, with an offset near black, so dark shadows don't turn into dark dots). Also sets how much Sharpening ignores as noise. Where the GPU has no float render targets, the older edge-aware filter (5×5, plus a coarse stage above 65) is used instead |
| Color denoise | 0–100. Colour noise only: the coarse blotches (8 px and up) by the same multi-scale denoise, the fine speckles by an edge-aware filter (radius 9 px, plus above 65 a coarse stage over 18 px). Colour is measured relative to brightness, so texture under strongly coloured light stays detail (a mono LUT on a blue-lit face no longer turns it blotchy). Independent of Luma denoise, so colour noise can go while fine luminance texture stays. Edits and presets from before the split had one Denoise for both: there Color denoise follows Luma denoise until it is moved |
| Sharpening | 0–100. Unsharp mask on luminance as a log ratio (σ 0.7 px vs 2 px), ignores differences below a noise threshold; no dark halos |
| Local contrast | −100–100. Mid-frequency detail: texture a few pixels across (bark, fabric, hair, rock), between Sharpening and High-pass contrast. Each pixel's luminance against an edge-aware blur (~5 px), so strong edges get no halo; large differences are compressed, so the boost goes to texture rather than outlines; deep shadows and near-clip highlights are left alone. − smooths texture |
| High-pass contrast | −100–100. Clarity-style local contrast against a wide blur (~1/170 of the long edge) from a 512 px copy of the whole photo, so preview, 100% and export match; mostly midtones |
| Skin retouch | 0–100. Finds skin with MediaPipe's selfie multiclass segmentation model (on device, first use per photo), then smooths blotches and redness edge-aware at ~1.2% of the face size while keeping pore texture; eyes, lips, brows, hair and background are untouched |
| Blemish removal | 0–100. Finds small round dark/red spots on skin (same skin mask, with eyes, brows, lips and nostrils cut out using MediaPipe's Face Landmarker; very dark spots such as moles are kept), scored by how much they stand out from the surrounding skin texture; the slider sets how faint a spot may be. Each is healed like a spot-heal brush: smooth tone fill from its surroundings plus skin texture from a clean patch nearby, feathered. Applied to the full-resolution image (undoable), so preview, 100% view and export match |
| Portrait retouch | 0–100 (web). A natural high-end skin retouch modelled on Reblum's Natural preset: **40 is about Reblum Natural at 40** (measured on the owner's before/after pairs), higher retouches more and catches fainter flaws, lower less. On all skin the segmentation model finds (face and body), sized by the face width so a headshot and a full-length portrait look alike: spots and small dark or red marks healed to their surroundings in brightness and colour (light bumps half), dark shading under the eyes lifted part-way, dark lines in the frown, forehead, nasolabial and marionette areas softened, and a light evening of mid-scale unevenness away from the skin's outline (half on the body). Pores and grain (under ~0.3% of the face width) are never touched; moles, tattoo ink, eyes, brows, lips and nostrils are kept. Worked out on the CPU from the preview (about a second, in the background; the slider shows the last result scaled meanwhile) as a map of gains the shader multiplies in, so preview, 100% view and export match. Needs a face (from the face landmarks, else the face-skin area) to size it by. Retouch preset group; Auto has it as the Adjust tab's Retouch slider |
| Bloom | 0–100. Diffusion-filter glow (core 0.30, halo 0.25 with warm rim, power-law tail 0.45 reaching ~10% of the long edge), round, dense next to the light and fading into a wide haze like a mist filter, energy-conserving, in scene-linear light with a pre-clip highlight boost so light sources glow; only highlights and the tones just below them glow (a smooth knee from 1 to 3 EV above middle grey, after Exposure), so midtones and shadows stay sharp and unveiled; the glow thickens as the slider rises, 75% of the light scattered at 100 (about a 1/2 black mist) |
| Anamorphic flare | 0–100. Fake anamorphic lens flare: horizontal streaks across the framed picture (keeps horizontal when rotated or straightened, unchanged by crop), scattered from small, very bright light sources only (street lamps, bulbs, headlights): a crisp needle and a wide wing reaching ~27% of the long edge on each side, with each colour channel reaching a different distance, so the streak runs from a white core through cyan to blue tips like a real coating. **Big lit windows and signs do not flare**, and neither do lights that do not blow out the sensor. Tap the name or raise above 0 to choose **Neutral**, **Blue** (classic cinematic cyan-blue), **Golden** (warm amber) or **Magenta** (purple coating), and switch on **Ghosts** (faint tall ovals mirrored through the frame centre, off by default); Detail preset group, carry over |
| Grain | 0–100. Film-style grain: randomly placed soft grains about 1/3200 of the long edge (fine-grain film) with grains a half and a quarter that size mixed in (down to about one pixel), so the texture stays crisp at any Size, mostly luminance, added in AgX's log (density-like) domain. Like film, it is even in the midtones and turns into sparse light specks in the shadows and sparse dark specks in the highlights. Tap the name to open **Size** (0–100, 25 = default, each 25 doubles it: 0 = half, 100 = 8×), **Roughness** (0–100: mixes in clumps twice the grain size, so the grain looks uneven) and **Color** (0–100: 0 = monochrome grain, 100 = each channel its own; default 15). They stay open while any of them is changed; Detail preset group, carry over |
| LUTs | Optional 3D LUT (HaldCLUT `.png` or `.cube`) on the final sRGB image, with Strength 0–100. Default: No LUT |
| Lens | Embedded lens profile (DNG opcodes: shading gain maps, WarpRectilinear, FixVignetteRadial), on by default when present. Manual **Distortion**, **Horizontal distortion** (cylindrical horizontal distortion: bows vertical edges while keeping horizontals straighter), **Fisheye** (all platforms; tap its name to open the lens settings, which stay hidden until you do or change one, like Grain's; 0–100 straightens: it turns a fisheye lens's picture towards a rectilinear one, assuming an equidistant fisheye; 100 stops short of full rectilinear, because a 180° view has no finite rectilinear picture. It auto-crops to real picture like Distortion, and also where the picture would be stretched more than 4× towards the edges (Crop below 0 shows those smeared edges); its **Lens field of view** (90–220°, 180 default) is the angle across the lens's image circle, or across the frame's diagonal when the picture fills the frame; for a **circular fisheye** **Find the circle automatically** (on by default; the corners must be dark and the picture round, otherwise nothing is found) measures the circle, or switch it off and set **Image circle size** (% of the frame's half diagonal) and **Circle centre X / Y** so they match the round picture, and the auto crop then stays inside it), **Crop** (−100–100: 0 auto-crops so no empty edges show, negative zooms out to show the curved black borders, positive crops tighter), Vignetting and Vignette radius. Chromatic aberration: **Red/Cyan** and **Blue/Yellow** scale the red and blue planes around the image centre (±0.2%, about ±6 px in the corners of a 24 MP photo), for raws without a per-plane warp in the file (Fuji RAF, Pixel DNGs); they add to the profile's own correction. **Save lens preset** keeps these settings as the lens preset for the photo's lens, under the name in the field next to it (the lens by default). Saving again under the same name replaces it; another name saves another preset, so one photo can save several. Manual lenses record no lens: name the preset yourself and pick it in Auto. Lens presets are listed with the other presets; **Send to Auto** (Android, iOS) also hands it to RawBoy Auto. On the web the Auto tab uses it directly. **Web only: Use Lensfun profile** (Lens tab, under the embedded profile) corrects distortion, chromatic aberration and vignetting from the Lensfun lens database, see Lens corrections |
| Crop & rotate | Top-bar button: 90° turns left/right, **⇆ Flip** / **⇅ Flip** (mirror the picture as shown left-right or top-bottom; the crop, straighten angle and masks mirror with it), a Straighten slider (±45°, smooth, auto-cropped), **Vertical** and **Horizontal** perspective sliders (−100–100, keystone correction as if the camera had been tilted by up to 30°: Vertical below 0 widens the top, for buildings shot looking up, above 0 the bottom; Horizontal above 0 widens the right side, below 0 the left; auto-cropped so no empty edges show, the centre stays put, and they turn and mirror with the picture), a **Scale** slider (100–200%, zooms the image within the full frame so the exported image preserves its original resolution) and a crop frame over the whole picture: drag corners, edges or the inside. Aspect ratios Free, Original, 1:1, 4:5, 4:3, 3:2, 16:9, 2:1, 2.39:1 with a Portrait/Landscape switch. The crop is applied last, so lens corrections and the auto crop always work on the full frame. Resets for each new photo |
| Presets | Top-bar button (opens in place of the slider tabs): save the current edits under a name, choosing which groups to include (Light, White balance, Curves, Color, Detail, Retouch, LUT, Lens; lens is off by default; web also has Denoise & sharpening, off by default, and Masks). **Select all** / **Deselect all** beside Include toggles every group at once. Tap a preset to apply it: only its groups change. Update, rename, delete; import/export as `.rawboy.json` files, the same format on Android and Web. **Send to Auto** (Android, iOS) opens RawBoy Auto with the preset applied to its whole batch. Rotation, flips, straightening, perspective, scale and crop are never part of a preset |
| Send to Auto (one photo) | Android: **Export › Send to Auto** opens RawBoy Auto with this photo and all its edits (web: **Send to Auto** / **Back to Auto** in the top bar). Auto develops it exactly as here, crop, masks with their brush strokes and Lens blur, and light leaks included, and its **Export all** renders it with the rest of the batch. For the same look on many photos use a preset instead: a photo's crop, strokes and leaks belong to it |
| Auto | Top-bar button: RawBoy Auto's automatic develop on this photo, without a look: exposure (faces steer it, phone DNGs keep the phone's own exposure), highlights, shadows, contrast, saturation, luma and color denoise from the noise measured in the photo, sharpening, white balance back to as shot, tone mapper AgX (a Film stock stays on, like the LUT). The LUT and everything else (crop, lens, masks, retouch, grain, colour grading) stay as they are. Reset goes back to as-shot |
| Histogram | Above the slider tabs: the developed photo's red, green and blue levels (from the 512 px preview copy), with the share of crushed shadows (left corner, every channel at 1 of 255 or below) and clipped highlights (right corner, a channel at 255). Tap it to paint those pixels blue and red on the photo; tap again to hide. Not in exports |
| Undo, Redo | Top-bar arrows (on phones at the end of the file name's line); Ctrl/⌘ Z and Shift Ctrl/⌘ Z on the web. Every edit of the open photo can be taken back: a slider drag, crop drag or brush stroke is one step, a button (Auto, Reset all, a preset, a LUT, a turn) is one too. Up to 100 steps; opening another photo starts afresh |
| Masks | Top-bar button (opens in place of the slider tabs): up to 4 layers, each a **brush** layer, a **linear** gradient (graduated filter: solid line full effect, dashed line none, drag the middle to move, an outer line to change width and angle, the square or the Curvature slider to bend the lines into arcs) a **radial** one (ellipse with centre, radius dots, rotation knob and a dashed feather ring) or a **stripe** (a soft band like the light leaks' streak, for a horizon, a road or a beam of light; centre, length and width dots, rotation knob, a dashed feather outline, Feather, and Bend (also the square on the band) and Taper as the light leaks have). Any layer can be painted on with **Brush** or **Erase** (Size, Hardness, Flow; **Auto mask** paints only on colours like the one under the brush), also carving parts out of a gradient; Undo stroke, Clear brush; keys B, E, V, `[` `]`, Z (100%, where painting works too), Space to pan. Each layer has Opacity, Invert, Show mask (red overlay), and its own Exposure, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Temperature, Tint (offsets from the global white balance), Saturation and Clarity, added to the global sliders inside the mask, plus **Portrait retouch** (web: the Detail tab's Portrait retouch only inside the mask, e.g. brushed over a face or an arm; it needs a face in the photo to size by, and adds to the global slider) and **Lens blur** (Android, web): a round, lens-like defocus inside the mask (up to 3% of the long side), where small bright lights become clean bokeh discs and the sharp part next to the mask is never smeared. Masks sit on the image, so they follow crop, straighten, rotation and lens corrections. While the tool is open the photo gets an empty margin, and the handles are drawn beyond the photo too. Reset for each new photo; a preset can include the gradients (Masks group, off by default); brush strokes stay with the photo |
| Light leaks | Top-bar button (Android, iOS, web; opens in place of the slider tabs): up to 4 film-style light leaks over the finished picture, e.g. to tastefully cover part of a nude, made like a Photoshop light leak: a white shape, blurred, with an orange then a red glow around it, all in **Screen** mode, so the photo shows through the glow and the white middle turns what is under it white (at 100% it hides it). The photo's grain stays on top of the leak. **+ Streak** (a beam of light across the photo, fading out past its ends), **+ Glow** (round) and **+ Triangle** add one; a switch turns the selected leak into another shape. On the photo: drag inside to move, the dots to resize (streak: side dots width, end dots length; triangle: apex, base and base corners), the round knob to turn, the square to bend; a glow (web) also has 8 hollow dots on its outline to drag it into any shape, e.g. around what it should hide (**Round outline** resets it). New leaks start organic (Warp 35, Texture 70, Cover 100); Android and iOS show Texture and Cover as sliders and draw a reshaped glow but can't reshape one yet. Sliders: **Intensity**, **Width** and **Length** (streak) or **Size** and **Stretch**, **Rotation**, **Bend** (curves it into an arc), **Taper** (narrower towards one end), **Warp** (an organic outline: noise pushes it around and grows lumps, only ever outwards), **Texture** (film burn: the light is exposed into the picture, so it burns white in the middle, yellow and orange around it and red at the edge, mottled and streaked, its colour drifting; 0 = the clean Photoshop layers), **Cover** (the photo in the middle of the leak, from its centre to about halfway to its outline, is replaced by a whitish wash under the light, and the middle burns whiter, so nothing shows through there however the texture falls; the leak's edge stays light over the photo; for hiding nudity on social media: make the leak a little larger than what it hides), **New pattern** (another warp and texture), **Softness** (the white core's blur), **Glow** (how far the coloured glow reaches), **Colour** (magenta-red to amber; the outer glow is always a redder version). They sit on the image like masks and follow crop, straighten, rotation and lens corrections. Reset for each new photo and never part of a preset; kept in the edits sidecars |
| Healing brush | Top-bar button (web; opens in place of the slider tabs): click or tap a dust spot, blemish or small object to heal it away. A spot is a circle (**Size**, as a share of the photo's long side, up to 3%; **Feather**) healed in the full-resolution image, so the export has it: the tone inside is filled in from around it (a Laplace membrane over the smoothed picture) and the texture is carried over from a donor disk, either the cleanest nearby one the engine finds (**Auto source**) or a dashed circle you drag onto an area of the same kind (**Choose source**, clone-stamp style). Drag a circle to move it (its source goes along), Delete removes it, `[` `]` change the size; up to 64 spots, each edit redoes the healing about 300 ms after you stop. Spots stay on the same content through crop, rotation and lens correction, belong to one photo (no carry-over, not in presets; edits sidecars and Auto's *RawBoy edits* keep them) and combine with Blemish removal. Undo and Redo work on them. Limits: spots near the photo's edge are healed from replicated edge pixels, large or detailed objects (a person, a branch) won't heal convincingly, and Auto's grid thumbnails (512 px) don't show them. |
| Effects | Top-bar button (web; Android and iOS: the Light button's Effects switch; opens in place of the slider tabs): up to 4 "lomo" light effects over the finished picture, after the light leaks, like light double-exposed onto film: **+ Lightning** (a forked bolt with a white-hot core), **+ Sparks** (an electric spark cluster: a hot centre, crackling arcs, flying sparks and soft orbs, in mixed blue, violet and green), **+ Trails** (long, nearly parallel streaks of moving light, flickering along their length) and **+ Swirl** (loose neon spirals). Every press draws a new random one; **New random** redraws the selected effect with the same settings, and a switch turns it into another kind. A new effect starts 1.75 times the size of the photo, centred on it, so most of it is outside the frame and only a part overlaps the picture (Size and Stretch reset to that by double-tap). On the photo: drag inside its ellipse to move it, the dots to size it along and across, the round knob to turn it. Sliders: **Intensity**, **Branches** / **Sparks** / **Strands** (how much there is), **Size**, **Stretch**, **Rotation**, **Thickness** (line width), **Glow** (how far the coloured glow and the soft halo reach), **Colour** (any hue) and **Colour mix** (how far each line may drift from it). The light is drawn as resolution-independent lines (sharp in a full-size export) and exposed into the picture per channel, so strong light burns to white with a coloured edge; the grain stays on top. They sit on the image like masks and follow crop, straighten, rotation and lens corrections. Reset for each new photo and never part of a preset; kept in the edits sidecars and in Android's Send to Auto edits. **Android and iOS (2026-11-10):** the same effects, patterns and defaults (the generator is a port, checked against the web's for the same seeds), reached from the **Light** top-bar button: its panel has a Light leaks / Effects switch, because the Android and iPhone top bars have no room for a seventh tool. The Effects panel has the same sliders; **Auto** shows them only in Send to Auto edits on Android |

The **?** button in the top bar of every app (Ctrl+/ or ⌘/ on the web) shows the basics and gestures in a short sheet.

RawBoy opens RAW files and, like Auto, JPEG, HEIC, PNG and TIFF (from the Open button, Photos on iPhone, "Open with" on Android, or a drop on the web). Those are already processed by the camera, so they give lower-quality results than RAW; with no edits a photo exports as itself (see [JPEG, HEIC, PNG and TIFF](auto.md#jpeg-heic-png-and-tiff)).

Edits carry over when you open the next file, except rotation, flips, straightening, perspective and crop, which reset per photo. White balance carries over only if you changed it; otherwise each file uses its own as-shot white balance. Reset returns to the file's as-shot defaults. Double-tap a slider (or tap its value) to reset it. On the web, the mouse wheel over a slider moves it without clicking (Shift: big steps, Ctrl/⌘: fine steps). Double-tap the image for a 100% view at that point, drag to pan, and double-tap again to go back. Pinch with two fingers to zoom around them, from a quarter of the whole photo (it stops once at the whole photo, so masks, light leaks and effects can be placed outside its edges) up to 400% (it stops once at 100%), panning with the fingers, and a double-tap from any zoom goes back to the whole photo; on the web the mouse wheel and a trackpad pinch do the same, and double-click or **Z** goes back to the whole photo. Press and hold the image to compare with the as-shot render. A thin frame around the photo (neutral grey by default) helps judge exposure, also in the 100% view; tap outside the photo (on the web, or press **D**) to cycle it through grey, white and black.

## LUTs

- **Included:** everything in `luts/` (`.cube`, HaldCLUT `.png`) is bundled at build time by the `copyLuts` Gradle task:
  15 film stocks generated by `uv run tools/film_luts/generate.py` (see [RawBoy Auto](auto.md); the script
  regenerates them, e.g. after a change to AgX in `shaders/develop.glsl`, whose inverse it contains), `Redscale.png`, `Movie Green.cube`,
  the colour-swap LUTs `Lagoon.cube`, `Violet Dust.cube` and `Unseen.cube` (`tools/swap_luts/generate.py`), and the film flash LUT
  `Film Flash.cube` (`tools/film_flash_luts/generate.py`).
  The tab lists them in three sections, **Color**, **Experimental** (Movie Green, Redscale, Lagoon, Violet Dust, Unseen) and **Black & white** (the
  monochrome looks' LUTs). The Presets panel
  likewise lists black and white presets (Saturation -100, or a black and white LUT at full strength) under their own
  heading.
- **Your own:** put `.cube` / HaldCLUT `.png` files in `Documents/luts` on the phone. In the LUTs tab, tap **Choose folder** once and allow access (the picker opens at that folder). The folder is rescanned every time you open the tab.
- While the **Film** tone mapper is on, the LUTs tab is hidden and the chosen LUT isn't applied (preview and export);
  it stays chosen and comes back with AgX, Filmic or Sigmoid. A note under the stock picker says so.
- LUTs larger than 64³ (e.g. a 4096 px HaldCLUT = 256³) are resampled to 64³ for the GPU.
- 1D `.cube` files and non-0..1 `DOMAIN_MIN/MAX` aren't supported.

## Lens corrections

- **From the file:** DNGs (Pixel and other phones, Adobe DNG Converter) carry lens corrections as opcodes. RawBoy reads them itself, because LibRaw ignores them:
  - *GainMap* (OpcodeList2): lens + colour shading per Bayer channel, applied at decode in camera RGB. Turning the profile off puts back only the luminance falloff, never colour blotches.
  - *WarpRectilinear* (distortion, and lateral CA when per-plane) and *FixVignetteRadial* (OpcodeList3), applied on the GPU.
- Files without this data (most camera-native raws) show "No lens correction data"; the manual controls still work (and, on the web, the Lensfun database below).
- **Lensfun database (web Pro, off by default):** the Lens tab's **Use Lensfun profile** switch applies the community-measured
  [Lensfun](https://lensfun.github.io) profile of the lens (distortion, lateral chromatic aberration, vignetting) in place
  of the file's own embedded one (the file's colour/lens shading from DNG gain maps stays), so it works for raws that carry
  none: Fuji RAF, Sony ARW, Canon CR2/CR3, Nikon NEF... The database (about 1,000 cameras and 1,500 lenses) is fetched only once the
  switch is on (a 27 kB index, then the calibration file of the one lens). Three ways to choose the lens:
  - **Auto** matches the camera and lens from the photo's EXIF (camera make/model, then the lens name, by letters and digits so
    "XF18-55mmF2.8-4 R LM OIS" finds "XF 18-55mm f/2.8-4 R LM OIS"; the camera's mount decides which lenses can be on it, and a
    weak name match is left unmatched rather than guessed). The focal length and aperture come from the EXIF and can be edited.
  - **Pick lens**: searchable camera and lens lists (type words, e.g. "sony 16 f/2.8"). With a camera chosen only lenses of its
    mount (and compatible adapters) are listed; tick *Lenses of every mount* for adapted lenses. Without a camera the sensor
    comes from the photo's 35 mm equivalent focal length. The pick stays for the next photos; Auto matches again.
  - **Generic**: a lens that is not in the database: **crop factor** (or the sensor's width and height in mm), **focal length**,
    **aperture** and **lens type** (rectilinear, or a fisheye: equidistant, equisolid angle, stereographic, orthographic), plus
    optional PTLens distortion terms a, b, c and vignetting k1, k2, k3 if you know them (e.g. from Hugin). A fisheye type
    shows its diagonal field of view and a **Straighten fisheye** button, which sets the existing Fisheye slider and field of
    view from it (the Fisheye code assumes an equidistant projection, so the other types are approximate).
  The profile follows Lensfun's rules (a calibration made on a smaller sensor than yours is not used; calibrations are
  scaled to your sensor's size and the lens's real focal length; between measured focal lengths the values are interpolated
  linearly, vignetting also by aperture and distance). It is fitted into the same structures as an embedded DNG profile
  (a per-colour-plane WarpRectilinear and a radial vignette; the fit stays within about a pixel at the corner of a 24 MP frame), so
  it works with the other lens controls: the manual Distortion, Fisheye, Crop, Vignetting and the Red/Cyan and Blue/Yellow
  sliders add on top, and the auto crop follows it. **Use embedded profile** switches the whole profile (Lensfun or file) off.
  Not kept in presets or edits sidecars; the switch and a generic lens are remembered. Lensfun's "acm" models, the
  distortion-spline interpolation and focus distance (always infinity) are not implemented.
  Data: Lensfun database, CC BY-SA 3.0, converted to compact JSON in `web/public/lensfun/`; the code is RawBoy's own (see
  [third-party.md](third-party.md)).
- **Chromatic aberration** (Red/Cyan, Blue/Yellow) samples the red and blue planes at their own scale around the image centre, after the DNG warp, in the same shader pass for the preview and export. It does not change the auto scale.
- **Distortion, Horizontal distortion and Crop:** Distortion warps radially; **Horizontal distortion** warps the horizontal axis (cylindrical distortion), bowing vertical edges while keeping horizontals straight. At **Crop** 0, the image auto-scales so no empty corners show (like Lightroom's Constrain Crop); negative values zoom out with anti-aliased black borders to keep the wide field of view, and positive values crop tighter. Output size is unchanged.
- DNGs decoded by the Adobe DNG SDK (JPEG XL DNGs) have their opcodes applied by the SDK itself, so the profile switch is unavailable for them.

## Output naming

`IMG_1234.CR3` → `Pictures/RawBoy/IMG_1234-rawboy.jpg`. If that name exists:
`IMG_1234-rawboy-001.jpg`, `-002`, … JPEG keeps the main EXIF fields (camera, lens, exposure, date, GPS).

**Film scan 1.5×** (Export dialog, Android and web; only while the Film tone mapper is on): renders the photo 1.5× larger. The photo is upscaled by iterative back-projection and lightly sharpened before the grain, and the film grain is generated at the finer pitch. The Size setting is ignored for a scan. Needs float render targets, otherwise it is a plain enlargement.
